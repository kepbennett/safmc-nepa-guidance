The Challenge

Why NEPA efficiency matters for the SAFMC

Fishery management actions are delayed by uncertainty over NEPA requirements, document complexity, and staff burden.

12--18 mo Typical timeline for an amendment with full EA
3--5 yr Potential duration when an EIS is triggered
Limited Council staff capacity for parallel NEPA workstreams
  • Timeline pressures -- actions lose relevance when NEPA review takes years
  • Document complexity -- unclear EA vs. EIS thresholds cause delays and rework
  • Staff burden -- each action requires near-duplicative baseline data collection
  • Underutilized tools -- tiering and NAPPs are authorized but rarely used by councils
  • Cross-council gaps -- shared stocks face duplicative NEPA processes across councils

Executive Summary

Three strategic recommendations

A practical framework for reducing NEPA timelines while maintaining environmental review quality.

1 Establish clear criteria for NEPA document types
2 Front-load scoping and baseline data to enable adaptive management
3 Improve cross-council coordination protocols for shared stocks
These recommendations reduce duplicative analysis, establish predictable timelines, and position the SAFMC as a leader in efficient NEPA compliance.

Recommendation 1

Clear Criteria for NEPA Document Types

Define when an EA, EIS, or Categorical Exclusion applies -- and adopt Supplemental Information Reports as standard practice.

EA vs. EIS Determination

  • Categorical Exclusion (CE) -- routine, minor actions with no significant impact
  • Environmental Assessment (EA) -- standard amendments with non-significant but documentable impacts
  • Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) -- major actions with potentially significant or controversial impacts

Key Process Improvements

  • Framework vs. Amendment clarity -- define which actions need framework vs. full amendment
  • Tiering -- tier from programmatic analyses to avoid repeating baselines
  • SIRs -- standard practice for minor modifications, avoiding full EA duplication
  • Data cutoffs -- firm deadlines to prevent perpetual document revision

Recommendation 2

Front-Load Scoping and Adaptive Management

NEPA Advanced Planning Procedures (NAPPs) build a reusable analytical foundation that streamlines every subsequent action.

The NAPP Framework

NAPP: A framework under PD 09-103 that front-loads environmental analysis -- building comprehensive baselines before individual actions are proposed, so subsequent actions reference existing work.

SIR: A brief document that incorporates new information into an existing NEPA analysis. When new data does not create previously unanalyzed significant impacts, the SIR documents that finding and no new EA or EIS is required.

  • Strategic EA/EIS design -- draft analyses broad enough to cover foreseeable actions
  • Standing baseline data -- maintain continuously updated environmental and fishery data
  • Tiering -- subsequent actions reference programmatic analyses rather than rebuilding them
  • Adaptive management alignment -- sync NEPA documents with stock assessment cycles

Legal Authority for NAPPs and SIRs

PD 09-103 provides specific procedures for both NAPPs and SIRs, confirms tiering authority, and provides detailed guidance on their application -- giving the SAFMC a clear, legally grounded pathway to implement every recommendation in this analysis.

NAPPs and SIRs are explicitly authorized by existing NOAA regulatory directives:

  • PD 30-132 (2013) -- originally formalized NAPPs and SIRs as fisheries-specific tools for adaptive NEPA compliance
  • PD 09-103 (July 2025) -- reissued after Trump administration EO rescinding CEQ's authority to regulate NEPA and shifting that authority to individual agencies. Clarifies and reinforces the 2013 rule's original intent for NAPPs, SIRs, tiering, and adaptive management
  • NAO 216-6A (2016, updated 2025) -- NOAA's overarching NEPA procedures, updated in response to the same EO. NAPPs and SIRs explicitly authorized in the NAO 216-6A Companion Manual (2017)

Recommendation 2 -- Phased Strategy

Near-Term and Long-Term Approaches

Two complementary pathways toward fully adaptive NEPA management.

Approach A: Near-Term

Strategic EA/EIS with SIRs, Tiering, and Adaptive Management

  • Design foundational EAs broad enough to support tiering
  • Subsequent actions tier from baseline, using SIRs as needed
  • Pre-analyze a range of management responses in baseline docs
  • Align refresh cycles with stock assessment schedules
  • Define clear criteria for SIR vs. new EA

Approach B: Long-Term

Programmatic EIS for Comprehensive Adaptive Management

  • Build a Programmatic EIS as the comprehensive analytical foundation
  • All subsequent actions tier from the PEIS with SIRs
  • Pre-analyze full range of management responses in the PEIS
  • Align PEIS refresh with stock assessment schedules
  • Precedent: Alaska Groundfish PEIS has served NPFMC for 20+ years

Recommendation 3

Cross-Council Coordination for Shared Stocks

When two or more councils manage the same stock, NEPA duplication multiplies. Formal coordination protocols can cut that burden.

Formal Coordination Agreements

  • Joint NEPA protocols -- MOUs designating a lead council for shared-stock NEPA
  • Coordinated science -- align stock assessment timelines and data across councils
  • Shared baselines -- maintain joint environmental documents both councils reference

Strategic Use of Framework Provisions

  • Compatible frameworks -- harmonize provisions so routine adjustments need one NEPA document
  • Cross-council tiering -- one council's programmatic analysis supports the other's review
  • Joint public comment -- coordinate scoping to reduce stakeholder fatigue

Recommendation 3 -- Case Studies

Shared-Stock Coordination in Practice

Two cases that illustrate both the challenges and opportunities for cross-council NEPA coordination.

Blueline Tilefish: SAFMC -- MAFMC

  • Background -- jointly managed by SAFMC and MAFMC with separate FMPs
  • Challenge -- each council prepares independent NEPA documents for overlapping actions
  • What's working -- joint SSC working group coordinates assessments and unified ABC recommendations
  • Shared foundation -- joint SSC puts all science in one place for shared decision-making
  • Next step -- formalize with standing working groups, SOPs, and MOUs for institutional continuity

Coastal Migratory Pelagics: SAFMC -- Gulf Council

Oldest collaborative fishery framework in the U.S. (est. 1983), jointly managing king mackerel, Spanish mackerel, and cobia.

  • Clear division: SAFMC manages Atlantic groups, GMFMC manages Gulf groups
  • Streamlined permitting: single federal permit covers king mackerel across both EEZs
  • Cooperative science: unified SEDAR assessments ensure quotas reflect biology, not boundaries
  • Coordinated upgrades: parallel FMP updates (e.g., Amendment 33) across both councils
  • Joint outreach: synchronized port meetings feed cohesive input into amendments

Path Forward

Phased Implementation and Measuring Success

An integrated strategy that builds momentum through early wins and scales toward long-term structural change.

Phase Actions Timeline
Phase 1 Adopt document-type criteria, SIR procedures, and data cutoffs; begin MOU talks 0--6 months
Phase 2 Pilot strategic EA for one complex; launch NAPP baselines; finalize first MOU 6--18 months
Phase 3 Expand EA/SIR model across complexes; implement tiering; formalize MOUs 18--36 months
Phase 4 Full adaptive management; cross-council tiering operational; SIRs standard 3--5 years

Measuring Success

  • Reduction in time from action initiation to NEPA completion
  • Share of actions processed via SIR vs. full EA
  • Decrease in duplicative baseline analysis
  • Successful cross-council coordination on shared-stock measures
